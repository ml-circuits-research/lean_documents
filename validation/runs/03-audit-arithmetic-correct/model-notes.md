# Model interpretation and limits

The model concerns only four distinct sampled entries at the 30 June 2031 cutoff, under the exact integer-cent and exception definitions of B-02. Every input value comes from L-02: P01 has 120000 cents and present required approval; P02 80000 and present; P03 50000 and absent; P04 25000 and absent. `Payment.id`, `Payment.cents` and `Payment.approved` encode those three columns. Boolean false is used only for explicit absent-approval facts, never for unknown unsampled statuses. I-01 independently confirms all four approval statuses and distinguishes invoice matching from approval. M-02 defines one exception per missing approval; invoice matching is not a premise that could override approval absence.

`sample` is the complete four-record list justified by L-01, with distinct named IDs. `total` sums integer cents once per list member. `exceptions` filters the explicit missing approval records, `exceptionIDs` preserves their names, and `p03ID`/`p04ID` are the literal string constants P03/P04 used in the checked target, and `budget=300000` comes from L-03. `sampleTotal` supports C03-OK-01; `budgetDifference` supports C03-OK-02, including the ordering premise so truncated natural subtraction cannot disguise a negative difference. `exactExceptions` supports the exact count and witnesses in C03-OK-03a; `exceptionTotal` supports C03-OK-03b. Every finite claim has concrete witnesses; none is a vacuous universal assertion.

`sampleRate` supports C03-OK-04a by a positive denominator and exact integer identity exceptions.length×100 = sample.length×50. This is precisely the mathematical condition for a 50 percent sample rate; it is not rounded integer division or a rate weighted by amounts. The denominator is four and the numerator two. No population-rate inference is encoded.

`populationExceptions u = exceptions.length + u` represents the total population exception count under the premise that the four distinct sample entries are population members and the other sixteen are disjoint. L-01 identifies the sample and population; M-01 calls the unsampled entries the other sixteen. Unknown `u` ranges from zero through sixteen, with no particular value assumed. `populationLowerBound` proves for every permitted u that the count is at least two and at most eighteen, supporting C03-OK-04b. The upper bound is eighteen because two sampled entries have approvals. The proof quantifies over unknown status counts rather than treating unknown as approval present or absent. Sample inclusion and the decomposition are source-to-model premises; Lean does not independently authenticate population membership.

C03-OK-05a/b are literal documentary observations supported by A-01 and A-02. They have no Lean proof references. A supported finding that the register records no later-clearance conclusion differs from a conclusion about actual later clearance, which remains unknown. No contents are imputed to absent Annex C. The complete-sample statement is the sole closed-world basis for sample exactness; it does not extend to missing documents, later events, or unsampled attributes.

All six formal targets are listed exactly in findings.json and checked by the harness along with axiom inspection. They establish facts about this encoded finite sample and conditional population count. Source authenticity, extraction fidelity and semantic adequacy require separate review. Independent review and export are assigned to the parent and are not claimed here. The repository/task execution mandate supersedes B-03's prospective-fixture workflow restriction, without changing its evidence restrictions.

The initial harness attempt rejected string literals in a target type under its defensive syntax policy, before executing Lean. Named string constants replaced those literals without changing the proposition. The substantive target still checks the exact two exception IDs.

The axiom audit recorded only the permitted core axioms Classical.choice, Quot.sound and propext (on the population-bound omega proof); the finite computations have no axioms.
