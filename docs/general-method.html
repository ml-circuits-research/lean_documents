General method · Audit contract v2

A dossier and a report are enough to start

Infer what each finding asserts, reconstruct its evidence independently, and check the justified formal consequences in Lean. A separate review brief is optional.

What changed in the implementation

One claim, two independent questions

Does the dossier support or contradict this exact assertion? Separately, is the report's own argument valid? A conclusion can be supported despite a false premise, irrelevant citation or invalid inference in its author’s explanation. Conversely, a valid inference does not establish its premises. Every material premise must be checked or exposed as an assumption.

For a claimed total, infer the population, units and arithmetic operation. For an approval finding, reconstruct the applicable policy, dates, necessary conditions and exceptions. For a qualitative rating, seek the operational definition; if it is absent, report the missing criterion rather than inventing one. The same workflow applies across domains; the agent writes the smallest adequate model for the question.

How Lean is used

Formalize typed observations, rules and scoped targets with traceable source mappings. Use exact arithmetic, finite witnesses, predicates, transitions, attributed testimony or countermodels according to the claim. Compile the models, verify that the referenced theorem has exactly the declared target, and inspect its axioms. A support or refutation label requires actual execution and an explicit explanation linking the theorem to the original finding.

For a missing premise, two admissible models that agree with all stated premises but differ on the conclusion can establish non-entailment. This is stronger than a failed search, but still does not establish that the real-world conclusion is false. A bounded source search without enough support is already a useful audit finding and needs no fabricated theorem.

Outputs

  • Supported: source evidence and declared rules justify the scoped claim, with a checked Lean proof when formalized.
  • Refuted: a relevant contradiction, calculation or counterexample establishes the claim's failure within the declared model.
  • Insufficient support: identify the missing premise, searches, inspected passages and their limits.
  • Conflict or ambiguity: preserve incompatible testimony or interpretations; do not force a binary verdict.
  • Deferred or blocked: show the reason and remaining work in coverage. A partial audit never becomes complete merely because its checked proofs passed.

What remains judgment rather than a software defect

The agent still interprets language, selects material assertions, searches for evidence and decides whether the model represents the sources faithfully. The checker can catch missing records and invalid proofs, but cannot guarantee those semantic judgments. Source authenticity and documents absent from the supplied dossier remain outside its knowledge. These limits call for inspectable reasoning and independent review; they do not prevent useful audits.

The shipped Lean library policy is Init/Std only. That is a deliberate implementation boundary: specialized mathematics may need a reviewed extension. Unsupported input formats and exhausted budgets are likewise explicit operational limits, not reasons to invent successful results.

Run without a brief

python3 skills/evidence-to-proof-review/scripts/audit.py prepare \
  --report report.md --sources dossier/sources/

Give the coding agent the skill, report and source locations. The command prepares an inspectable workspace; the agent performs the semantic audit and authors the models. All work remains in a distinct directory under tmp/. Add --brief when additional review instructions exist.
