Acceptance contract

A verdict must explain its reasons

The useful unit of output is an explained finding audit, not a green compilation badge. Missing arguments are valuable findings even when the truth of the original claim remains unresolved.

Mandatory explanation for every finding

  1. Original finding: exact report wording, position, ID, and atomic claim being assessed.
  2. Verification problem: the question, intended proposition, domain, version, time, and what would count as support or refutation.
  3. Evidence used: exact source passages and positions, attributed speakers, opposing evidence, missing documents, and search boundaries.
  4. Rules and premises: applicable criteria, exceptions, interpretation decisions, assumptions, and their individual justification.
  5. Argument: readable numbered steps connecting premises to the conclusion; each step identifies the supporting evidence, rule, or theorem.
  6. Outcome: evidence and formal states, precise failure or success, proof target/artifact if one exists, and anything not established.
  7. Next action: corrected wording, the evidence that would resolve the gap, or a targeted clarification. Avoid unconditional recommendations resting on disputed premises.

Three different negative results

Claim refuted

Claim: “Every listed invoice is at most 100 units.” The complete listed table contains invoice X for 120. X belongs to the claimed population; 120 > 100. This witness contradicts the universal statement. A formal check may verify membership, comparison, and the universal negation.

Argument invalid

Claim: “The release is ready because its smoke tests passed.” The cited rule requires both smoke tests and security approval. The argument omits a conjunct. A countermodel can show non-entailment; this does not establish that the actual release is unready.

Support not found

Claim: “No approval existed.” The supplied export contains no approval attachment, but its coverage is explicitly incomplete. Report INSUFFICIENT_SUPPORT: the absence of an attachment is established; absence of approval is not. Specify what was searched and what record is needed.

Support-gap reporting is a deliverable

Distinguish not justified by the report's own citations from no sufficient support found after reviewing the supplied corpus. An auditor may find an independent justification for a poorly cited but correct claim. In that case report both the citation defect and the recovered supporting argument.

For each gap, record the missing premise or inference, supplied citations examined, additional sources searched, search completeness and limits, and why the available facts do not bridge the gap. Distinguish absent evidence, inaccessible evidence, ambiguous evidence, an incomplete search, and a proof attempt that ran out of resources. None is automatically evidence of falsity.

Formal acceptance gates

  • The exact target corresponds to the supplied claim, or is explicitly labeled as a narrower auxiliary result.
  • The model records domain, applicability, time, source mapping, assumptions, and exceptions; contradictions are isolated and vacuity is checked.
  • Build the modules containing every published theorem, from identified sources and locked dependencies; record actual commands, outcomes, and tool versions.
  • Reject incomplete proofs, unapproved axioms, and kernel bypasses. The initial policy permits only audited standard Lean axioms where needed and disallows custom axioms.
  • Inspect theorem dependencies, imports, and theorem statements independently of a text search for forbidden tokens. A premise equivalent to the desired conclusion fails semantic review even if the proof compiles.
  • A refutation must prove the negation of the exact claim or verify a relevant counterexample. An unverified witness is a candidate, not a formal refutation.
  • Record the semantic review and the level of checker assurance actually achieved. A checker that is missing or incompatible is marked unavailable.

Technical reference and trust boundary

The official Lean proof-validation reference distinguishes proof acceptance from the meaning of the theorem. It describes #print axioms, including dependencies on sorryAx and custom axioms. Its standard axiom examples are propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound. It also describes lean4checker --fresh, which replays compiled proofs through Lean's kernel; this is additional checking, not an independent implementation of the kernel. For stronger adversarial checks it describes comparator workflows and external checkers. Tool availability and compatibility must be tested against the selected version before the project promises these checks.

Project policy adds a separate source-to-model review and a human-readable argument. Neither a successful build nor a checker can establish source authenticity, resolve an ambiguous domain rule, or certify the original report as a whole.

Report-level summary

Count claims by evidence outcome, argument quality, formal result, and coverage independently. Show formalized claims / eligible claims alongside reviewed claims / total material claims. State exclusions and resource-limited checks. Never present reviewed-but-unresolved as proved coverage. A report containing a refuted finding is not thereby false in every finding.
