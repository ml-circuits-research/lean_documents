Model selection

Formalize the question, not the whole document

Choose the smallest model that preserves the meaning needed to audit a finding. More formalized text does not automatically give a more trustworthy conclusion.

StrategyGood useProposed Lean representationCritical limitation
Finite data and computationCounts, totals, duplicate IDs, exact thresholds, bounded membership.Records, finite lists/sets, integer minor currency units, decidable predicates with kernel-checked proof terms.Correct computation over incorrectly extracted data remains wrong about the source. Predicates and conditional rulesTrigger → obligation, applicability, exceptions, role separation.Typed entities and propositions, explicit hypotheses, separate trigger and exemption predicates.A sufficient condition is not an equivalence. An unproved antecedent cannot establish the conclusion.
Time and effective versionsApproval before execution, deadlines, policy transitions.Normalized instants or calendar-day indices; explicit time zone, interval endpoints, and version selection.Calendar vs working days, inclusive boundaries, holidays, and time zones must come from the mandate or rule.
State-transition modelsAllowed workflow or narrative transitions; object continuity.States, events, transition relation, finite traces, invariants.Persistence and impossible transitions need an explicit domain rule. Fictional resurrection is not forbidden by default.

A practical selection sequence

  1. Split a compound finding into atomic claims while preserving the original wording and quantifiers.
  2. Define what would count as support and as refutation. Identify required evidence and disputed terms.
  3. Try a finite check first; move to predicates, traces, or argument graphs only where needed.
  4. Search for relevant counterexamples alongside attempts to establish the claim.
  5. If a definition or premise cannot be responsibly fixed, retain the documentary argument and mark the formalization limitation.

Search engines propose; proof checks decide

Text search, Datalog, and SMT may discover candidate rules, witnesses, and models. Their output must be linked to input records. An SMT “unsat” message alone does not receive a Lean-proof label. Replay a certificate through an approved checker or reconstruct the exact obligation in Lean. Record the solver version, query, result, and trust boundary when a non-Lean method is reported.

Worked design example: procedural approval

The supplied report says “All reviewed operations complied.” The intended target is a universal proposition over the declared operation set, with the applicable policy version and exceptions. A refutation needs one operation in that set, a rule requiring prior approval for it, proof that no exemption applies, and reliable evidence that prior approval was absent or too late.

Report claim:       ∀ x ∈ reviewedOperations, Complies x
Candidate witness:  operation B
Required facts:     B ∈ reviewedOperations
                    ruleApplies B
                    noApplicableException B
                    explicitEvidenceOfMissingPriorApproval B
Proposed result:    ¬ (∀ x ∈ reviewedOperations, Complies x)
Separate case C:    no approval record found → support gap, not breach

This is a proposed obligation, not executed Lean code. The initial specification's Boolean classification lemma is useful as a small unit property, but it cannot replace this source-to-witness argument.
