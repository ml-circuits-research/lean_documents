1# Response review correspondence and arithmetic clarifications23All organizations, people, agreements, events, and rules in this source are synthetic.45<a id="F01"></a>6## [F01]78The response analyst's draft timeline converts A's detection from 02:00 +02:00 to 00:00 UTC and its containment from 03:20 +01:00 to 02:20 UTC. The elapsed interval is therefore one hundred forty minutes. An early margin calculation obtained eighty minutes by subtracting the two printed local labels; the analyst struck it out after noticing the different offsets. This arithmetic note corroborates the timestamp conversion rule rather than establishing a new clock convention. The relevant distinction is between explicit offsets, not an assumption about how a particular country's daylight-saving schedule normally works on that date.910<a id="F02"></a>11## [F02]1213The analyst adds A's approved nonoverlapping pause durations, thirty plus forty, obtaining seventy raw approved minutes. Because A's accepted qualifying detection is exactly the revision 2 boundary, only forty-five minutes are excluded from the containment metric. The resulting active-response interval is ninety-five minutes, which exceeds the ninety-minute P1 allowance by five minutes. This calculation treats approval validity and combined exclusion cap as separate questions. Both pauses can be validly approved even though their full combined duration is not deductible. Replacing the cap with sixty minutes would improperly apply revision 1 to A.1415<a id="F03"></a>16## [F03]1718For B, the review note retains 03:10 UTC as actual qualifying detection but uses 03:20 UTC, the authorized MB-27 window end, as the effective acknowledgement-clock start. Delivered acknowledgement at 03:45 UTC is twenty-five minutes after that start, within the thirty-minute allowance. The note explains that a thirty-five-minute raw detection-to-acknowledgement interval is not inherently a failure when an express scoped deferral applies. The same note explicitly rejects applying any maintenance deferral to A because A is priority P1. It does not claim that the existence of planned work alone establishes any other incident's compliance.1920<a id="F04"></a>21## [F04]2223The review coordinator lists the three A escalation results separately for discussion. The technical-lead delivery happened eleven minutes after detection; the commander delivery happened twenty-two minutes after detection. The executive trigger occurred at 01:00 UTC because A remained uncontained, and the executive delivery followed twelve minutes later at 01:12. The coordinator asks for corrective process discussion but makes no claim that one timely stage cancels another stage's delay. The underlying register is the evidence of events; this correspondence is a calculation and interpretation aid, not an authoritative alteration of delivered timestamps.2425<a id="F05"></a>26## [F05]2728A manager describes the response in an email as “professionally managed and reassuring.” A shift employee calls the early escalation delays avoidable. Neither provides an agreed scoring rubric, weighting of outcomes, or definition of professional management. Those views can be included as attributed opinions, but the objective timeliness record alone does not determine every broader evaluative adjective. The correspondence also distinguishes the unresolved C containment disagreement from the separate missing A monitoring period. One is conflicting testimony about a specific event; the other is unavailable observational evidence, and neither should be compressed into a claim that every supplied incident fact is unreliable.
