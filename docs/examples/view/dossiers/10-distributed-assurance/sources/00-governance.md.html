1# Synthetic Lantern Relay governance23[G01] Finite identity universe. The invented Lantern Relay service contains exactly sixty nodes, N01 through N60, during September 2034. The node registry is complete for identities, not automatically for all events or all permit histories. Each operational record states its own evidence completeness. A first retrieval window is not the full corpus. Universal assertions require every applicable node and exception, not extrapolation from the first files found. All services, principals, signatures, bundles, and incidents are synthetic; this packet does not prescribe administration of a real distributed service.45[G02] Authorization by revision. Policy revision B becomes effective at 00:00 on 15 September. A release at or after that instant requires an active operator credential at release time, a signed approval by a principal with scope over that node at approval time, and an unrevoked permit covering the release. Before that instant, revision A requires an active operator credential and signed local-supervisor approval; no central permit is required. Event time controls historical applicability. Report date, file modification date, and discovery date do not replace the operative timestamp. Successful technical handoff does not itself establish administrative authorization.67[G03] Hierarchy and identity. Resolve aliases through the dated identity registry. A reused display name does not transfer the former principal's credential. Supersession replaces only the named scope from its stated effective instant. A valid board exception can modify the ordinary route if its node, time, and other conditions match. The board's power to grant exceptions does not establish that a requested exception was granted. Ordinary approval authority does not include authority to rewrite policy. Filename ordering creates no precedence. The applicable hierarchy must be assembled before deciding whether the ordinary permit requirement governs a particular release.89[G04] Missing and contrary evidence. A missing permit attachment in an expressly incomplete history establishes a missing argument, not proof that no permit existed. A complete bounded permit history can establish absence within its specified interval. An explicitly revoked permit with a statement that no replacement existed is different from an empty search result. One documented unauthorized release refutes a global claim that all recorded releases were authorized, even if other cases remain unknown. Completeness of the node domain does not establish completeness of all release events. A claim of exactly one event needs event-history evidence, not merely a valid authorization for one known event.1011[G05] Snapshot provenance. An audit result is bound to exact source bytes, report bytes, inventory, extraction assumptions, and reviewed formal premises. Reuse after a source change requires checking affected premises. An unchanged report hash or unchanged numerical value is insufficient if scope, authority, effective time, or exception text changes elsewhere. Preserve the original snapshot and logs. Reproduction belongs in a distinct repository tmp/ directory with a new source manifest and fingerprint. A prior proof must not be described as validation of changed inputs without reviewing its dependencies. This rule is evidence bookkeeping, not permission to overwrite the baseline corpus.
