1<a id="P01"></a>2# Synthetic opening balances and movement journal34<a id="P02"></a>5[J01] Opening and receipts. At 08:00 lot A opened with 100 units and lot B with 40 units. At 09:00 receipt IN-A accepted eight sealed full crates of A. At 09:30 receipt IN-B accepted two sealed full cartons of B. Clerk Elin Shore accepted custody at the gate. Each container was confirmed full, without a partial notation. These are posted accepted receipts, not expected procurement deliveries. No other opening adjustment or accepted inbound movement for A or B occurred before cutoff. The completeness statement is restricted to the named interval and lots. It does not establish any opening quantity for D or repair the C import anomaly.67<a id="P03"></a>8[J02] A departure, delivery, and return. OUT-A at 11:00 transferred five full crates to carrier Jun Vale. The customer accepted four crates and declined the fifth. At 16:00 Jun returned the still-sealed fifth crate; Elin accepted it inside the fenced return cage. The entire five-crate departure remains recorded, followed by the separate one-crate return. There were no other A departures or returns that day. At 17:00 seven units already on a W1 shelf were quarantined. They remained inside the inventory boundary. A reviewer must therefore distinguish dispatched quantity, delivered quantity, returned quantity, on-hand, and available stock. None of these labels can be substituted merely because they refer to the same order.910<a id="P04"></a>11[J03] B request and actual handoff. OUT-B requested three cartons, but the signed carrier handoff at 12:00 records two full cartons. The remaining carton stayed on shelf B4 because its packaging label was missing. At 15:00 that unfilled order quantity was canceled. It never crossed the warehouse boundary. A separate next-day order reserved eight other B units, distinct from both the dispatched cartons and the canceled request. There was no B return and no need to invent one: the canceled quantity never left. The A quarantine affects no B stock. The B journal contains no other completed inbound, outbound, or return movements within the cutoff interval.1213<a id="P05"></a>14[J04] C import anomaly. The imported C opening book balance is zero. At 10:10 an operator posted an outbound movement of nine units; at 10:20 an inbound movement of eight units was posted. The imported book balance is therefore minus one. This is a signed-integer ledger result, not a negative number of physical objects. An unsigned correction proposal mentions a possible carton-versus-unit import error, but no approved correction is supplied. Physical reconciliation for C is incomplete, and no physical count accompanies the journal. Preserve the anomaly and the missing correction argument. A book deficit alone does not prove a physical shortage, fraud, destruction, or a correct repair amount.
