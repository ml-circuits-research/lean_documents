A receiver message tentatively proposes 74 units for transfer D-17, but it is unsigned and marked provisional. A carrier message says that the faint copy looks like 78. Neither is a signed correction. The original scanned quantity line is pale, and its second digit is hard to read. No customer acceptance signature appears beside that quantity. There is no original carbon copy or later authenticated reconciliation in the packet. The two interpretations are not a license to select the convenient value. Even a legible planned quantity would not automatically establish the quantity actually accepted by the receiver.67<a id="P03"></a>8[M02] A completion confirmation. The customer accepted four A crates and declined one. The carrier returned the declined crate to North Quay before cutoff, and the warehouse acknowledged acceptance inside the cage. Thus every crate that departed has a documented later disposition, but the customer did not receive all five. A claim that the order was completely delivered differs from a claim that the movement chain can be traced. The return closes custody for the fifth crate while preserving the distinction between delivery and return. A reviewer should quote the relevant stage and quantity instead of using the vague term completed without defining what completed means.910<a id="P04"></a>11[M03] B order closure. The customer confirms receiving the two cartons actually transferred to the carrier. The third requested carton remained at the warehouse and was canceled. All handed-over cartons were delivered, but the original three-carton order was not fully delivered. These statements concern different denominators and are consistent. Cancellation closes unfilled demand without moving stock. It does not prove a customer return, loss, or hidden replacement shipment. The separate eight-unit reservation remains an allocation within the warehouse and must not be confused with the canceled carton. Each statement retains its lot and order identity.1213<a id="P05"></a>14[M04] Missing supporting material. A useful finding identifies precisely which missing record could resolve the uncertain quantity. Missing evidence does not itself establish that the carrier short-shipped, that the customer miscounted, or that the document was forged. No external retrieval is authorized by this finite packet; an audit should retain the bounded evidence gap.
