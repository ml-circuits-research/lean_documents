1# Synthetic signed count sheets23[N01] Elin Shore signed COUNT-A-1 at the 18:00 cutoff. The stated count is: Lot A, all W1 shelves and fenced return cage, total 148 units. It includes the seven quarantined units and the returned sealed crate. Open shelves were counted by individual units; unopened crates used the verified standard conversion. No damaged, returned, or reserved goods were excluded. A witness initial appears, but the witness did not supply an independent count. The sheet is accepted as signed evidence within this synthetic packet, not as an infallible world axiom. Its numerical agreement with book arithmetic does not automatically invalidate another equally authoritative observation.45[N02] Tomas Reed independently signed COUNT-A-2 for the same 18:00 cutoff, same lot, and same boundary. The stated total is 147 units. The sheet expressly includes quarantine and the return cage. Reed has the same authority as Elin for this count. A margin note mentions a loose sleeve under a bin but does not state whether it was counted. That incomplete note cannot be used to rewrite the signed total. No signed reconciliation, recount, or authorized cancellation resolves the discrepancy. Both count sheets remain active evidence. Neither the order of their filenames nor the auditor's preferred arithmetic gives one count source priority over the other.67[N03] A separate B control sheet records 40 units physically inside the boundary, including its eight reserved units. It does not count C or D. Agreement between this B count and B journal arithmetic does not resolve the A discrepancy or the C import problem. No one signed a statement that all lots were reconciled. The A difference is one unit rather than a rounding difference because units are exact integers. A reviewer should distinguish a claim about what each signed sheet records from a claim selecting the true physical A total. The first can be well supported even when the second has conflicting evidence.89[N04] Count custody. The count sheets were transcribed into this source from synthetic signed forms and checked for matching lot, cutoff, and boundary labels. Their recorded signature names are evidence inside the invented exercise; no real-world handwriting examination is claimed. The transcriber was not authorized to correct observed quantities. The source does not establish which observer made a mistake or whether a sleeve moved during counting. A physical recount and a signed reconciliation would address that uncertainty. Until then, retaining both observations is more faithful than erasing a disagreeing one or deriving unrelated conclusions from inconsistent premises.
