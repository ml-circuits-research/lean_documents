# The Name on the Crate

## S01 — Three people

The harbor warehouse opened under a sky the color of worn pewter. Inside, Mira brushed salt from the office windowsill while her sister Iona searched for a dry pencil. Their father had once rented this building, and neither sister liked the new brass number fixed above its door.

Tomas, the porter, arrived carrying the morning register. These were the only three people physically present anywhere on the warehouse property between 08:50 and 09:10 that morning: Mira, Iona and Tomas. In the register Mira used the signature “M.” The initial was her own abbreviated name, not another person or a second identity.

## S02 — The divided building

The property contained an upper loft and a lower quay, two distinct places connected by a staircase. At any one instant a person could occupy only one of them. The morning described here allowed no exception to that ordinary arrangement.

At exactly 09:00, the narration finds Mira alone in the upper loft. She stood beside a closed blue crate and read the chalk marks on its lid. Iona was in the ground-floor office. Tomas was on the lower quay, checking a rope that had hardened overnight. These are the positions at that instant, not descriptions of where the three stood earlier or later.

## S03 — The register

A page in the register bore the names “Mira,” “M.,” “Iona” and “Tomas.” Mira's full name appeared in the arrival column; her initial appeared beside a correction to yesterday's weight. Iona had signed for the office keys. Tomas had entered the delivery time.

When Iona noticed the correction, she held the book closer to the window. The changed figure made the final line look crowded. She asked whether the crate should be opened before the driver returned. Mira came down from the loft but did not answer. Instead, she wiped a patch of chalk dust from her thumb.

## S04 — Tomas's account

Later that morning Tomas said, “At exactly 09:00, I saw Mira standing beside me on the lower quay.” He meant Mira herself, the same woman who signed “M.,” and he meant this morning's 09:00, not another minute or another day.

Iona asked whether he might have mistaken the time. Tomas repeated his statement. The narration records his insistence without endorsing the claimed sighting. His statement is testimony by a character; it does not relocate Mira from the loft where the narration placed her at that instant.

## S05 — The unopened lid

After Tomas left, Iona laid a screwdriver beside the crate. Mira looked at it, then at the window. A gull landed on the roof and walked above them with the sound of someone testing loose floorboards.

Neither sister opened the lid before the morning scene ended. Mira's silence remained unexplained. The crate's contents were not revealed. The chalk dust on her thumb showed that she had touched chalk, but the narration gave no account of who had changed the marks or why the lid remained shut.
