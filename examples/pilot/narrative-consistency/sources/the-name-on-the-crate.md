# The Name on the Crate

## S01 — Chapter 1: Three names at the door

The harbor warehouse opened under a sky the color of worn pewter. Inside, Mira brushed salt from the office windowsill while her sister Iona searched for a dry pencil. Their father had once rented this building, and neither sister liked the new brass number fixed above its door. The old number had left a darker rectangle on the wood; from the road, the two shapes almost looked like a correction.

Tomas, the porter, arrived carrying the morning register beneath his coat. He shook the rain from the cover before setting it down. A corner had swollen where water had entered between the pages, and he pressed it flat with the heel of his hand. Iona found a pencil in the drawer where their father had kept spare buttons. Its point snapped as soon as she began to sharpen it.

Between 08:50 and 09:10 that morning, Mira, Iona and Tomas were the only three people anywhere on the warehouse property. The gate stayed empty after Tomas came through it. The driver had gone away earlier; his van was no longer by the wall. Beyond the boundary, cranes moved over another yard, their hooks rising without any load that the sisters could see.

Mira signed the register with the initial she always used for corrections: “M.” It was her own abbreviated name. Iona watched the familiar stroke and pushed the blotting paper toward her. When they were children, Mira had practised a much more elaborate signature, with a line beneath it. Nothing of that flourish remained except the long final stroke of the M.

Tomas took the book outside to check an entry against the delivery board. The sisters could hear his boots on the lower steps, followed by the scrape of the board against its hook.

## S02 — Chapter 2: Above the quay

The warehouse had been enlarged in pieces. Its upper loft reached over the ground-floor office, while a staircase descended along the outer wall to the lower quay. Rainwater collected in a groove on the lowest step. Anyone carrying a box had to turn sideways at the bend, lifting it over a rail polished by years of wet sleeves.

The loft and the quay were separate places. At exactly 09:00, Mira was alone in the upper loft, beside a closed blue crate. Iona was in the ground-floor office, and Tomas was on the lower quay, checking a rope that had hardened overnight. The bell from the harbor clock sounded through the open stairwell.

Mira read the chalk marks on the lid. One line was faint, another pressed heavily enough to leave a small ridge of powder. A piece of sacking lay between the crate and the wall. She drew it clear with the edge of her shoe and folded it over a beam, exposing a patch of floor that had stayed dry while the surrounding boards darkened.

Below, Iona pulled a drawer free of its swollen frame. Inside were invoices tied with string, a brass fastener and a photograph of the building when its sign still carried their father's name. She put the photograph on top of the invoices without turning it over.

On the quay, Tomas worked the stiff rope between his palms. Its end had frayed into short pale threads. He wound a length around the post, unwound it, and shook the water from his hands. From there he could see the loft window, but the glass returned the gray reflection of the sky.

## S03 — Chapter 3: The crowded line

A page in the register bore the names “Mira,” “M.,” “Iona” and “Tomas.” Mira's full name appeared in the arrival column; her initial appeared beside a correction to yesterday's weight. Iona had signed for the office keys. Tomas had entered the delivery time. The ink had dried to different shades, although the columns were ruled with a single firm hand.

When Iona noticed the correction, she held the book closer to the window. The changed figure made the final line look crowded. Beneath the page, older entries showed through the thin paper. She slid a blank sheet behind it, separating one day's handwriting from the next, and read the line again.

“Should we open the crate before the driver comes back?” she asked.

Mira came down from the loft but did not answer. Instead, she wiped a patch of chalk dust from her thumb. A little of it remained beside the nail. Iona left the question where it was and set the register on the desk, holding it open with the brass fastener she had found in the drawer.

A gust came through the stairwell. The blank sheet lifted, dropped, and lifted again. Mira put a cup on its corner. There was a chip in the rim, shaped like a small white tooth. Neither sister had ever remembered to throw that cup away.

Tomas returned the delivery board to its hook. Its lower edge struck the wall twice. He asked where the pencil had gone, and Iona gave him the broken one. He used the point that remained, turning the pencil after every few letters.

## S04 — Chapter 4: What Tomas remembered

Later that morning, Tomas leaned the register against the edge of the desk and said, “At exactly 09:00, I saw Mira standing beside me on the lower quay.”

Iona looked toward the stairwell. “Are you sure of the time?”

“Nine,” he said. “This morning. The clock was striking. Mira was beside me down there.” He pointed to Mira, the same woman whose full name and abbreviated signature appeared in the register. “You, not somebody walking past the gate.”

Mira stood near the office window. A drop ran down the glass between her reflection and the cranes beyond it. Iona asked once more whether Tomas might have mistaken the time. He repeated his statement, slowly this time, as if the spaces between the words could settle the question.

The broken pencil lay across the open book. Tomas picked it up, looked at its short point, and put it down again. He said the rope on the quay would need replacing before the next load. Iona asked whether there was another coil in the store cupboard. He said there might be, although the last one he had seen was too thin.

The conversation moved to the cupboard and the key that sometimes caught in its lock. No one supplied a reason for the difference between the remembered sighting and the morning's arrangement of people. Tomas buttoned his coat from the bottom upward, missing the first hole and having to begin again. When he lifted the register, the brass fastener fell to the floor. He bent to retrieve it and left it beside the cup.

## S05 — Chapter 5: The unopened lid

After Tomas left, Iona carried a screwdriver up to the loft and laid it beside the crate. Mira looked at it, then at the window. A gull landed on the roof and walked above them with the sound of someone testing loose floorboards. Dust shook from a beam and settled on the folded sacking.

Iona touched the wooden handle of the screwdriver. It had a dark mark where their father used to rest his forefinger. She moved it away from the edge of the beam so that it would not roll. Below them, a door swung against its catch and stopped. The wind had changed direction; the rain now struck the opposite side of the building.

The blue paint on the crate was scratched at its corners. One nail stood a little higher than the others. Neither sister opened the lid before the morning scene ended. Mira's silence remained unexplained, and whatever lay inside stayed out of sight. The chalk on her thumb showed that she had touched chalk; it did not tell Iona who had changed the marks on the lid.

The gull lifted from the roof. For a moment its shadow moved across the window, then the glass held only the colorless sky. Iona went downstairs and closed the register. Upstairs, the screwdriver remained beside the crate, its handle pointing toward the stairs. There was still room on the beam for someone to put down a hand.
