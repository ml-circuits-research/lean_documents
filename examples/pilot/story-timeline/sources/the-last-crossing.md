# The Last Crossing

## S01 — The clock above the door

At eight that evening, the river looked like a strip of unpolished metal. Rain trembled on the ferry office windows. Mara set two cups beside the stove, although she had been drinking alone since the winter timetable began. Across the water, a lamp burned in the locked workshop where her brother once repaired boats.

The office clock showed twenty minutes past eight when Mara stepped out. It was 20:20. She took the narrow footbridge toward the workshop, carrying an empty canvas bag. The office door swung shut behind her. This was her only departure from the office that evening before her return described below.

## S02 — The crossing

Halfway across, Mara stopped to free a strand of wool caught on a nail. The river sounded heavier beneath the bridge than it did from the office. She wondered whether the workshop lamp had been left on deliberately, then disliked herself for turning a light into a message.

At the workshop she tried her old key. It did not turn. Through the window she could see a chair, a coil of rope and a white envelope on the bench. She could not read the writing. She stayed outside, with rain running from her sleeves into her hands, and eventually turned back without the envelope.

## S03 — The return

Mara re-entered the ferry office at 20:55 that same evening. The same clock stood above the door; it had continued running correctly. The bag was still empty. One cup had developed a pale skin, and she carried both cups to the sink before taking off her coat.

A bus passed without stopping. Its windows briefly laid squares of yellow light across the floor. Mara watched those squares move away and thought of the envelope remaining dry on the bench. She left her key on the desk, beside the folded winter timetable.

## S04 — What Elias said

Elias, the night watchman, arrived later. He had been in the storeroom during Mara's absence. Looking at her wet coat, he said, “You came back at 20:10. I remember because the news had just begun.” He explicitly meant the return from this evening's single walk to the workshop, not a visit on another day.

Mara looked up at the clock. She neither agreed nor corrected him. Elias filled the kettle and began describing a road closure. His claim remained his own recollection; the narration does not adopt it as a second account of the actual return time.

## S05 — The untouched key

When Elias left, Mara moved the key from the desk to the windowsill. She did not throw it away. Nor did she go back across the bridge. The workshop lamp was still visible, a small yellow rectangle suspended above the black water.

She switched off the office light and stood for a moment beside the dark window. From the road, anyone looking in would have seen the kettle, the timetable and the outline of a woman waiting. What she intended to do with the key was left unsaid.
