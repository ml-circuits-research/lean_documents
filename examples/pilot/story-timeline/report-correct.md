# Reading the clock in The Last Crossing

This report examines the walk's chronology and the limits of interpreting Mara's response.

## R01 — Length of the absence

Mara's absence from the ferry office lasts 35 minutes.

The departure is 20:20 and the return is 20:55 on the same evening. Subtracting 20 minutes from 55 gives 35 minutes. This is the office-to-office interval, not a measurement of uninterrupted walking. [Departure, S01](sources/the-last-crossing.md#s01--the-clock-above-the-door), [return, S03](sources/the-last-crossing.md#s03--the-return), and [time conventions, S06](sources/reading-conventions.md#s06--time-and-attribution).

## R02 — An incompatible recollection

Elias's claimed 20:10 return time is incompatible with the established chronology of Mara's single walk.

He identifies the same walk, but 20:10 precedes its 20:20 departure and differs from the narrated 20:55 return. This identifies an inconsistent recollection, not a contradiction in the narrator's own event sequence and not proof that Elias knowingly lies. [The recollection, S04](sources/the-last-crossing.md#s04--what-elias-said), [departure, S01](sources/the-last-crossing.md#s01--the-clock-above-the-door), and [attribution rules, S06](sources/reading-conventions.md#s06--time-and-attribution).

## R03 — The meaning of silence

Mara's silence does not establish that she intends to deceive Elias.

She neither agrees nor corrects him; her intentions are not stated. Silence could accompany several motives, and the later placement of the key does not select one. The text therefore supports restraint about intention, rather than a finding that she is certainly honest or dishonest.
